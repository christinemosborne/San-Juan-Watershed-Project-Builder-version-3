Step 2 of 7
Before You Apply
Check these 6 things before you start applying for funding. Click each one to read more — then mark it done when you're ready.
A
Know where your project is located
▾

Before you look for funding, you need to know exactly where the project is and what kind of land it's on.

  • Is it in the Lower San Juan watershed? (This matters for some grants)
  • What kind of land is it — tribal trust, allotted, private, state, or federal?
  • Does the project cross from one type of land to another?
💡 If you cross from one jurisdiction to another, you'll probably need approval from both governments — but there may also be more funding available.
B
Know what type of project it is
▾

Different types of projects match different funding programs. Pick the category that fits best:

  • Drinking water or wastewater for homes and communities
  • Irrigation or livestock water (canals, ditches, wells, stock ponds)
  • Grazing land and rangeland health
  • Erosion and sediment washing into washes or the San Juan River
  • Riparian restoration (plants and habitat along streams)
  • Planning, monitoring, or climate resilience
💡 If it fits more than one category, that's OK — you may be able to bundle funding from multiple programs.
C
Line up your local approvals
▾

Most grants require that the chapter or community has already agreed to the project. Ask yourself:

  • Does the chapter need to pass a resolution supporting this project?
  • If it's on grazing land — do you need the grazing committee or individual permittees to agree?
  • Are there any known conflicts — like a lease holder, a right-of-way issue, or a sacred site nearby?
💡 Having a chapter resolution in hand before you call an agency makes a big difference — it shows the community is behind the project.
D
Check if a plan or study already exists
▾

Projects that come from an existing plan are easier to fund. Check if you have any of these:

  • A watershed plan, drought plan, or conservation plan that mentions this location or problem
  • Any past engineering reports, water quality data, or range assessments
💡 If you don't have a plan yet — that's OK. Some programs pay for planning first. Look for programs labeled "planning" or "technical assistance" in the Funding Directory.
E
Be honest about staff time and match
▾

Two questions to answer honestly at the chapter level:

  • Who will manage the grant? Someone needs to handle reports, invoices, and communication with the funder.
  • Can you provide match? Many programs require you to cover part of the cost — this can be cash, staff time, equipment use, or volunteer labor.
💡 If staff time is your biggest barrier right now, start with capacity-building programs like GAP, Section 106, WMPPD, or TCR — these can pay for a coordinator to help get other projects funded.
F
Call the right people early
▾

This guide helps you prepare — but it doesn't replace talking to people. Before you apply:

  • Contact your tribal program staff (water, environmental, agriculture, planning, or grants departments)
  • Contact the closest agency field staff (NRCS office, Bureau of Reclamation, EPA tribal coordinator, or state program contacts)
💡 Your opening question: "I have a project idea. I found your program in the Lower San Juan Watershed funding guide. Is this a good fit for our situation, and what should we do first?"
